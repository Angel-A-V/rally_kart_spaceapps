Lunar Model Wiring

Everything electrical sits on the fixed base. One controller (the Arduino UNO Q) drives the disc stepper and the Earth arm servo, and reads two sensors. Both motors get their power from the power module through a soldered PCB protoboard, which carries one shared 5V line and one shared GND line. Every ground is joined. The Sun lamp is a battery keychain light, so it has no wires at all.

Wiring diagram

5 V (power module) 3.3 V (UNO Q) GND (all joined) stepper signals home sensor light sensor Earth arm servo optional knob

Coloured tags are net labels: every tag with the same name is the same wire network. All GND tags are joined on the protoboard's GND line, with one wire from that line to a UNO Q GND pin. The 5V tags are the protoboard's 5V line, fed by the power module only; nothing connects to the UNO Q's 5V pin.

Four rules that protect the board

Motor power comes from the module

The stepper pulls about 240 mA and the servo spikes higher when it starts moving. A UNO Q pin can't supply that and can be damaged trying.

Join the grounds

Two power sources, one ground. Without the GND jumper the signals have no reference and the motor twitches or does nothing.

Never join the two 5 V supplies

Leave the UNO Q's 5V pin empty. Two supplies on one wire push current into each other.

Analog inputs stay at 3.3 V

Power the photoresistor and knob from 3V3. Analog pins A0 and A1 can't take 5 V at all, so they're not used here.

Bench tests to start now

Tests 1 to 4 need only a multimeter and the parts, no code and no UNO Q. Test 5 uses the spare ELEGOO UNO R3 and the kit's ready-made stepper example, so nobody writes code.

1 · Power module gives 5 V

Darren
  1. Connect the module's +5V and GND to the protoboard's 5V and GND lines. Plug in a USB phone charger.
  2. Set the jumper for that output to 5 V and switch the module on (its LED lights).
  3. Multimeter on DC volts: red probe on the 5V line, black on the GND line.
  4. Switch to continuity (beep): the UNO Q GND pin, the servo's brown wire and the ULN2003 − should all beep against the GND line.

Pass: 4.8 to 5.2 V, and every ground beeps. Note the voltage; it's the reference for every later test.

2 · Hall sensor sees the magnet, and how far away

Darren + Gustavo
  1. Hall VCC to the 5V line, GND to the GND line. If it's the bare 3-leg chip (A3144), add a 10 kΩ from OUT to 5 V for this test only, since there's no UNO Q pull-up yet. A KY-003 module doesn't need it.
  2. Multimeter between OUT and GND. With no magnet it reads about 5 V.
  3. Bring the magnet toward the sensor's printed face. When it switches, the reading drops near 0 V (a module's LED also lights).
  4. Flip the magnet over. Only one face works with an A3144; mark that face with a marker before it's glued into the disc.
  5. Measure with calipers the largest gap where it still switches reliably.

Gives Gustavo the magnet pocket orientation and depth, and Darren the sensor mount gap (target 2 to 5 mm). Send Angel the switching distance.

3 · Photoresistor sees the lamp and the wall's shadow

Darren
  1. Build the divider: one photoresistor leg to a 3.3 V source, the other leg to a 10 kΩ that goes to GND. For a bench test without the UNO Q, the module's other output set to 3.3 V works.
  2. Multimeter between the photoresistor/10 kΩ junction and GND.
  3. Read it three times: room light, keychain light shining on it from 120 to 150 mm away (the planned lamp distance), and with a card or the printed wall blocking the light.

Pass: lit and shadowed readings differ by at least 0.5 V. If not, try a 4.7 kΩ or 22 kΩ instead of 10 kΩ and tell Angel which works best.

4 · Keychain light is steady enough to be the Sun

Gustavo or Darren
  1. Pick the mode you'd use and leave it on for 15 minutes. Many keychain lights dim after a minute or two or switch off on a timer.
  2. Check the beam: a small, hard light gives the sharpest shadow behind the terrain wall.
  3. Measure the LED's diameter and body size for the lamp-tower holder, and how the button is reached when it's mounted.
  4. Note how long a charge (or battery) lasts, so it's full for the demo.

Feeds Darren's lamp tower: the LED centre goes at the height in the lamp table in cad/design_notes.md (33 mm for a flat horizon).

5 · Stepper turns the disc through the gears

Darren + Angel
  1. Wire section 2 of the build sheet, but to the ELEGOO UNO R3 instead of the UNO Q (same pins D8 to D11). Keep the motor on the power module and join the grounds.
  2. Angel loads the Arduino IDE's built-in stepper example (File → Examples → Stepper) with the pins set to 8, 10, 9, 11, in that order, which suits the 28BYJ-48.
  3. The ULN2003's four LEDs blink in turn and the shaft turns. Then mount the pinion and check the ring gear doesn't skip.
  4. Mark the disc and count motor turns for one disc turn: it should be 6.

Confirms the 6:1 ratio, the reversed direction and the mount's stiffness before the UNO Q is involved.

6 · Servo moves without hitting the stops

Darren + Angel
  1. Wire section 3 of the build sheet. Take the arm off the horn first: the IDE's Sweep example swings 0 to 180°, which would drive the arm into its hard stops.
  2. Angel loads File → Examples → Servo → Sweep with the pin changed to 6. The bare horn should sweep smoothly.
  3. Run the stepper test at the same time and watch the horn. If it twitches, add or enlarge the capacitor.
  4. Put the arm back on at horizontal near mid-travel and give Angel the direction that raises it and where the hard stops sit.

Pass: smooth sweep on D6 with no jitter while the stepper runs. Arm limits for the firmware: −52° to +48°, stops a few degrees outside.

Still to confirm

  • The three new parts. Angel's handoff says he bought a Hall sensor and a magnet. The Amazon links couldn't be opened from here, so the exact Hall part (bare A3144 chip or KY-003 module), the magnet size and the third part are unconfirmed verify. Both Hall versions use the same three wires.
  • UNO Q pins. Pin names follow the UNO header; that D2 takes 5 V and the ULN2003 switches reliably on 3.3 V signals both come from Arduino's UNO Q docs and common practice, and need a bench check verify.
  • Servo pin. D6 assumes the UNO Q marks it PWM (~) like the UNO R3 verify. Angel's firmware still needs the servo and the EARTH command.
  • Lamp angle. This guide puts the lamp 180° from the Hall sensor; docs/handoffs/angel-to-hardware.md says 0°. The firmware's home offset depends on which is right verify.
  • Charger rating. At least 1 A, 2 A preferred, now that the servo shares the 5V line with the stepper verify.
  • Keychain light specs. No datasheet found for the LIGHTFE E2, so test 4 is how we learn its runtime and modes.
  • Stretch parts (LCD1602, buttons) are left off on purpose. They can be added later without changing anything above.
